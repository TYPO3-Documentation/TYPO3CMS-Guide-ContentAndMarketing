=======================
What Content Goes Where
=======================

The TYPO3 project has four main websites. This page explains what kind of content goes where and, in general terms, who is responsible for that content.

The four websites are:

* `typo3.com <https://typo3.com>`__ — Explain and promote TYPO3 as a product and professional solution.
* `typo3.community <https://typo3.community>`__ — Introduce people to the TYPO3 community and help them connect with and participate in it.
* `typo3.org <https://typo3.org>`__ — Explain and build confidence in the TYPO3 Project, its organization, and long-term sustainability.
* `news.typo3.com <https://news.typo3.com>`__ — News and events about and concerning TYPO3 and what happens in the TYPO3 ecosystem.

With very few exceptions, all four sites contain only original content produced by the TYPO3 Association, the TYPO3 Company and their partners, or members of the TYPO3 Community.

.. tip::

    To publish content on news.typo3.com, please refer to :ref:`How to Get an Article Published in TYPO3 News <get-news-published>`.

.. _content-for-typo3-com:

typo3.com (Choose)
==================

:Core Question:
    Why TYPO3? (Could TYPO3 be the right solution for me?)

:Content Lifecycle:
    Evergreen; product and marketing

:Funnel Position:
    Awareness → Consideration → Conversion

:Main Audience:
    Decision-makers, businesses, marketers, digital leaders, agencies

:Topics:
    Product capabilities, use cases, solutions, services, partners, certifications, training, customer success, business benefits

:CTA Examples:
    Try TYPO3, find a partner, get in touch

:Editorial Responsibility:
    TYPO3 Company

This website positions itself around the product and its professional offerings, including support, integrations, certifications and training.

It is designed to move visitors moving people from “I have a digital/content-management problem” to “TYPO3 might be a suitable solution” and eventually toward a concrete action.

Content belongs here when its primary purpose is to communicate:

* What TYPO3 CMS is and what it does
* Business and organizational use cases
* Enterprise, public-sector and other solution scenarios
* Product capabilities and differentiators
* Professional services and support
* TYPO3 Company Partners
* Certifications and training
* Customer success and case studies

typo3.org (Trust)
=================

:Core Question:
    Can I trust TYPO3? (Who stands behind TYPO3, and why should I trust this project?)

:Content Lifecycle:
    Evergreen; institutional/project content

:Funnel Position:
    Trust → Commitment

:Main Audience:
    Existing users, Association Members, contributors, stakeholders, journalists, interested public

:Topics:
    Organization, governance, strategy, finances, membership, policies, project structure, official information

:CTA Examples:
    Become an Association member, learn about the governance

:Editorial Responsibility:
    TYPO3 Association

This website the is place for information about the TYPO3 project's governance and the Association's role (by extension also the TYPO3 Company), while directing product-oriented visitors to typo3.com.

It is less about selling the CMS and more about explaining the project behind it. It provides the institutional context that makes an open-source project credible and sustainable. It becomes particularly important after initial awareness, when people want to understand who is behind TYPO3, how decisions are made and whether the project is trustworthy for long-term use.

Website topics include:

* The TYPO3 Project and its structure
* TYPO3 Association and TYPO3 Company governance
* Governance and decision-making
* Strategy and long-term direction
* Association membership
* Finances and funding
* Policies and legal information
* Official project information

typo3.commmunity (Participate)
==============================

:Core Question:
    How can I be part of TYPO3? (How can I participate and find my place in TYPO3?)

:Content Lifecycle:
    Evergreen; participation/community content

:Funnel Position:
    Engagement → Participation → Advocacy

:Main Audience:
    Contributors, developers, editors, community members and newcomers

:Topics:
    How to contribute, teams, community groups, communication channels, collaboration, mentoring, community culture

:CTA Examples:
    Join the community, ask the community, contribute to TYPO3, share your knowledge, join a sprint

:Editorial Responsibility:
    TYPO3 Association

This website's funnel starts where the conventional customer funnel ends: Someone has moved from being interested in TYPO3 to wanting to use, contribute to, learn from or connect with the community.

TYPO3 describes participation as extending far beyond coding. Contributors include people who write documentation, develop software, organize events, translate, work on accessibility, participate in governance, mentor others, etc.

The content answers questions such as:

* How can I contribute?
* Where can I meet other TYPO3 people?
* Which teams and committees exist?
* How do I get involved?
* Where do discussions happen?
* How can I learn from others?
* How can I organize or join community activities?
* What opportunities are there for contributors?
* What are the community's values and ways of working?

news.typo3.com (Follow)
=======================

:Core Question:
    What is happening? (What's happening in TYPO3 right now?)

:Content Lifecycle:
    Time-bound; editorial content

:Funnel Position:
    Awareness → Engagement → Retention

:Main Audience:
    Entire TYPO3 ecosystem and interested outsiders

:Topics:
    News, announcements, releases, technical developments, project stories, market topics, success stories, event reports, opinions, events

:CTA Examples:
    (Any from the above sites)

:Editorial Responsibility:
    TYPO3 Company (shared with TYPO3 Association)

This site is the dynamic editorial layer connecting the other three sites. News is about things that happen, while the other sites primarily provide the stable context around those things. It helps maintain awareness and engagement among people who already know TYPO3, while also creating content that can attract people who do not.

It's all about about communicating developments, stories, and events, while remaining a repository of historical articles that may still be relevant or draw visitors.

The news categories should encompass all of TYPO3:

* Best Practice & Learning
* Developer & Technology
* Market & Sell
* Personal Stories & Opinions
* Product Updates
* Showcase & Success
* The Project

Additionally, two categories serve specific and utilitarian purposes:

* Newsletter
* Security

Association and Company Content Responsibilities
------------------------------------------------

In general terms, the TYPO3 Association is responsible for community-contributed articles and the TYPO3 Company for content created by or for their internal content department. In practice, this divides up many of the content topics too.
